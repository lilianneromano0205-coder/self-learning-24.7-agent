## PART 1 — Verification of the named works

### 1.1 Park et al. — "Generative Agent Simulations of 1,000 People" (arXiv 2411.10109)
- **Citation (verified):** Joon Sung Park, Carolyn Q. Zou, Jonne Kamphorst, Niles Egan, Aaron Shaw, Benjamin Mako Hill, Carrie Cai, Meredith Ringel Morris, Percy Liang, Robb Willer, Michael S. Bernstein. arXiv:2411.10109. v1 (15 Nov 2024) is titled "Generative Agent Simulations of 1,000 People"; **the current v3 (28 Jun 2026) was retitled "LLM Agents Grounded in Self-Reports Enable General-Purpose Simulation of Individuals."** Cite the arXiv id, not the title, to avoid confusion. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.10109
- **Summary:** 1,052 US participants each gave a ~2-hour semi-structured voice interview (v1: avg ~6,491 words transcript; recalled) plus surveys. An LLM agent is conditioned on the full transcript (and/or survey answers) and asked to answer held-out instruments as that person: GSS items, Big Five, five behavioral-economics games, and five experimental studies. The same participants re-took the instruments **two weeks later**, giving a per-person self-consistency ceiling.
- **Normalization (the key mechanism):** normalized accuracy = (agent-vs-participant-wave-1 accuracy) / (participant wave-2 vs wave-1 accuracy). For correlational outcomes: normalized r = r(agent, participant) / r(participant t1, participant t2). A value of 1.0 means the model predicts the person as well as the person predicts themself two weeks later; values >1 are possible if the agent regresses toward the person's stable mean better than the noisy re-take.
- **Key numbers (v3 abstract, verified):** on held-out GSS items, interview-only agents = **83%**, survey-only = **82%**, interview+survey = **86%** of the test-retest ceiling, vs **74%** for demographics-only agents. v1 headline: **85%** (interview agents; raw agent accuracy ≈ 68.85% vs participants' own two-week replication ≈ 81.25%; demographic-only ≈ 0.71, persona-description-only ≈ 0.70 — v1 numbers recalled, not re-read this pass). v1 also reported Big Five normalized correlation ≈ **0.80** (raw r ≈ 0.78 vs self r ≈ 0.95) and economic games normalized ≈ **0.66** (recalled). Agents replicated **4 of 5** experimental studies, the same count as the human participants themselves (recalled). Interview conditioning reduced demographic-parity accuracy gaps across race and political ideology relative to demographic-only agents (v3 abstract confirms "reducing accuracy disparities across racial and ideological groups").
- **What predicted well / poorly:** Attitudinal self-report items (GSS, Big Five) were predicted best; **behavioral economic-game choices worst** (≈0.66 normalized) — consistent with a general pattern that narrated self-description transfers to attitudes far better than to incentivized behavior. v3 notes predictive gains "begin to asymptote once the model has observed sufficient evidence" — extra data sources give only modest increments.
- **IMPLEMENTATION HOOK:** (a) Store every elicitation as JSONL rows `{item_id, wave, answer, ts}`; compute per-person self-consistency by re-asking a random 10–20% of items ≥2 weeks apart; report every fidelity metric as `model_acc / retest_acc` so the benchmark has a person-specific ceiling. (b) Separate the benchmark into "attitude" vs "incentivized behavior" strata and report both — expect the behavior stratum to be the hard one. (c) Use the "asymptote" finding to justify a stop rule for evidence ingestion (see Part 2B).

### 1.2 "Modeling Others' Minds as Code" — ROTE (ICLR 2026)
- **Citation (verified):** Kunal Jha, Aydan Yuenan Huang, Eric Ye, Natasha Jaques, Max Kleiman-Weiner (UW / JHU). arXiv:2510.01272; accepted ICLR 2026; Best Paper at NeurIPS 2025 LAW workshop. URLs: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01272 , https://openreview.net/forum?id=vHXo7xIer6
- **Summary:** ROTE = Representing Others' Trajectories as Executables. Treats action understanding as program synthesis: an LLM proposes a hypothesis space of ~30 executable Python programs (finite-state-machine style: internal states, state→action policy, transition rules on observations) that could explain an observed trajectory; Bayesian inference then weights programs by how well they reproduce the observed actions; prediction is the posterior-weighted vote.
- **Mechanism (verified from HTML):** posterior p(λ | h_0:t-1) ∝ Π_i p(a_i | o_i, λ) · p_prior(λ), with likelihood (1−ε) when program λ predicts the observed action, ε/(|A|−1) otherwise; prior from LLM token probabilities; Sequential Monte Carlo with rejuvenation (resample/replace low-weight programs by re-prompting) as more observations arrive.
- **Key numbers:** ~**50%** relative improvement over behavior cloning and LLM-prompting baselines. Construction gridworld (10 hand-designed FSMs, 50k state-action pairs): 0.522 vs 0.310 (best LLM baseline). Partnr embodied household sim (19 tools), single-step: 0.537 vs 0.333. Programs transfer to novel environment configurations without retraining (p<0.001).
- **IMPLEMENTATION HOOK:** Represent a person's habits as a list of small **rule programs** stored as JSON (`{id, when: predicate-string, then: action, source: "llm|hand", weight}`), evaluated by a tiny stdlib interpreter (or `eval` on a restricted AST). Keep a particle set of K rule-sets; on each observed decision, multiply each particle's weight by (1−ε) or ε/(|A|−1); renormalize; when effective sample size < K/2, drop the bottom half and ask the LLM for replacements conditioned on the mismatching observations. This gives calibrated posterior over "which script this person is running".

### 1.3 PAHF — "Learning Personalized Agents from Human Feedback" (arXiv 2602.16173)
- **Citation (verified):** Kaiqu Liang, Julia Kruk, Shengyi Qian, Xianjun Yang, Shengjie Bi, Yuanshun Yao, Shaoliang Nie, Mingyang Zhang, Lijuan Liu, Jaime Fernández Fisac, Shuyan Zhou, Saghar Hosseini. Submitted 18 Feb 2026. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.16173
- **Summary:** Framework for continual per-user personalization built as a loop of three components: (1) **pre-action clarification** (ask before acting when uncertain), (2) **grounding actions in retrieved per-user preference memory**, (3) **post-action feedback integration** that writes back to memory. Evaluated on two benchmarks (embodied manipulation; online shopping) that measure both how fast preferences are learned and how well the agent adapts when preferences change.
- **Key numbers:** Abstract claims the explicit-memory + dual-feedback-channel agent "learns substantially faster and consistently outperforms" memory-less and single-channel baselines, with lower initial personalization error and faster adaptation to preference shifts. Exact deltas not read in this pass (UNVERIFIED numbers).
- **IMPLEMENTATION HOOK:** Two JSONL feedback channels — `clarifications.jsonl` (question, answer, confidence-before) and `corrections.jsonl` (action, user-override, delta) — both feeding a `preferences.json` store keyed by (domain, slot). Trigger the clarification channel only when the policy's predictive entropy exceeds a threshold (Part 2B), so questions are rare.

### 1.7 "From Storage to Experience" (ACL 2026 Findings)
- **Citation (verified):** Jinghao Luo, Yuchen Tian, Chuxue Cao, Ziyang Luo, Hongzhan Lin, Kaixin Li, Chuyi Kong, Ruichao Yang, Jing Ma. ### 1.9 Lazzati & Metelli — IRL with closed-form reward centroids
- **Citation (partially verified):** Filippo Lazzati, Alberto Maria Metelli. "Generalizing Behavior via Inverse Reinforcement Learning with Closed-Form Reward Centroids." arXiv:2509.12010 (15 Sep 2025). URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.12010 . **Venue "PMLR v300, 2026" UNVERIFIED** — the arXiv page lists no venue; dblp entry for Lazzati (https://dblp.org/pid/345/8703.html) should be checked. Related follow-up: "IRL without an Optimal Demonstrator: A Feasible Reward Set Approach," arXiv:2605.30903.
- **Summary:** IRL is ill-posed: the **feasible reward set** is all rewards consistent with the observed expert policy. Rather than pick one, choose the "average" policy among those induced by rewards in a bounded subset of the feasible set; show that this policy equals planning with the **centroid** of that subset, derive the centroid in closed form, and give a provably efficient estimator from offline expert demonstrations only.
- **Identifiability implication:** rewards are not identifiable from behavior alone; what is identifiable is the feasible set. Any "cloned utility" must therefore be reported as a set/interval, and decisions in novel situations should be made by the centroid (or reported as ambiguous when the set is wide).
- **IMPLEMENTATION HOOK:** For a tabular decision domain, store the feasible set as linear constraints `Q(s,a*) ≥ Q(s,a)` in JSON; approximate the centroid by sampling reward vectors uniformly in a bounded box, keeping those that satisfy the constraints (rejection sampling, pure Python), and averaging. Width of the accepted set per state = your ambiguity flag.

## PART 2 — Mechanisms by topic

### 2A. Preference learning from choices

**A1. Bradley & Terry (1952) / Luce (1959) choice model.** R.A. Bradley, M.E. Terry, "Rank Analysis of Incomplete Block Designs: I. The Method of Paired Comparisons," Biometrika 39(3/4):324–345, 1952 (https://doi.org/10.2307/2334029); R.D. Luce, *Individual Choice Behavior*, Wiley 1959. Standard references, not re-fetched (well-established).
- Mechanism: P(i ≻ j) = exp(s_i) / (exp(s_i)+exp(s_j)) = σ(s_i − s_j); Luce generalizes to a choice set: P(i | C) = exp(s_i)/Σ_{k∈C} exp(s_k). MLE for scores via the Zermelo/MM iteration: s_i ← log( w_i / Σ_j n_ij /(exp(s_i)+exp(s_j)) ), where w_i = wins of i, n_ij = comparisons of i vs j. Converges when the comparison graph is strongly connected; add a small Dirichlet/ridge prior otherwise.
- IMPLEMENTATION HOOK: `choices.jsonl` rows `{ctx, options:[...], chosen, ts}`. Maintain `scores.json` per (domain, option-feature); run 50 MM iterations in pure Python on each nightly batch; expose P(chosen) as the per-choice fidelity probability, which feeds the calibration metrics in 2C.

**A2. Ziebart, Maas, Bagnell, Dey, "Maximum Entropy Inverse Reinforcement Learning," AAAI 2008, pp. 1433–1438.** https://cdn.aaai.org/AAAI/2008/AAAI08-227.pdf (verified via search).
- Mechanism: reward linear in features, r(τ)=θ·f(τ); trajectory distribution P(τ|θ) = exp(θ·f(τ))/Z(θ). Gradient of the log-likelihood of demos = empirical feature expectation − model feature expectation: ∇ = f̃ − Σ_τ P(τ|θ) f(τ). Model expectations computed by a soft value iteration ("backward pass": V(s)=log Σ_a exp(Q(s,a)), Q(s,a)=θ·f(s,a)+Σ_s' T V(s')) then a forward pass for state visitation frequencies D(s). Resolves the ambiguity of "which policy" by maximum entropy — the least-committal distribution matching feature counts.
- IMPLEMENTATION HOOK: for small discrete contexts (e.g. "reply now / later / never" × context features) implement soft value iteration with `math.exp`/`math.log` over dicts; features are booleans/counts from the event log; gradient ascent with a fixed step; store θ in `reward_theta.json` with the feature names. Zero dependency on numpy for ≤ few thousand states.

**A3. Boltzmann-rational (softmax) action models.** C.L. Baker, R. Saxe, J.B. Tenenbaum, "Action understanding as inverse planning," Cognition 113(3):329–349, 2009. https://web.mit.edu/9.s915/www/classes/cognition2009.pdf (verified via search).
- Mechanism: P(a | s, θ) = exp(β·Q_θ(s,a)) / Σ_a' exp(β·Q_θ(s,a')). β (rationality/inverse temperature) is itself a per-person parameter: β→0 uniform/random, β→∞ perfectly optimal. Goals/rewards are inferred by Bayes: P(θ | actions) ∝ Π_t P(a_t|s_t,θ) P(θ). This is the "Boltzmann-rational" assumption that underlies A4 and most reward learning.
- IMPLEMENTATION HOOK: fit β jointly with θ by grid search over β ∈ {0.5,1,2,4,8} maximizing held-out log-likelihood; report β as an interpretable "how consistent is this person" number and persist it; a drop in fitted β over time is itself a drift signal.

**A4. Jeon, Milli, Dragan, "Reward-rational (implicit) choice: A unifying formalism for reward learning," NeurIPS 2020.** https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2020/hash/2f10c1578a0706e06b6d7db6f0b4a6af-Abstract.html (verified).
- Mechanism: every feedback type (demonstration, pairwise comparison, correction, turning the robot off, language, proxy rewards) is a Boltzmann-rational choice from a (possibly implicit) choice set C with grounding function ψ: C → distributions over trajectories: P(c | θ) = exp(β·E_{τ~ψ(c)}[r_θ(τ)]) / Σ_{c'∈C} exp(β·E_{τ~ψ(c')}[r_θ(τ)]). The two design decisions for a new feedback channel are *what was the choice set* and *what does each choice ground to*.
- IMPLEMENTATION HOOK: define one JSON schema `{channel, choice_set:[...], chosen, grounding:{choice→[trajectory_ids]}}` for every signal (accepted draft, edited draft, ignored suggestion, "no, don't"). One likelihood function then updates the same θ posterior regardless of channel — this is how corrections and observed decisions can share a model.

**A5. Jarrett, Hüyük, van der Schaar, "Inverse Decision Modeling: Learning Interpretable Representations of Behavior," ICML 2021, PMLR 139.** http://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/jarrett21a/jarrett21a.pdf (verified).
- Mechanism: forward problem = a *normative* planner parameterized by (reward, plus bounded-rationality knobs); inverse problem = fit those parameters to observed behavior. Their instantiation, inverse bounded-rational control, quantifies interpretable notions — flexibility of decisions (entropy regularization), tolerance for surprise (KL to prior beliefs), optimism in beliefs — as fitted scalars. Generalizes IRL/imitation as special cases.
- IMPLEMENTATION HOOK: expose 3–4 named scalar knobs per person (β from A3; a "prior-adherence" term; a "risk-optimism" term); fit by coordinate grid search on held-out likelihood and store them in `profile.json` with confidence intervals from a bootstrap over days. These are the human-readable "personality parameters" of the clone.

### 2B. Active preference elicitation (when to ask, and when to ask "why")

**B1. Houlsby, Huszár, Ghahramani, Lengyel, "Bayesian Active Learning for Classification and Preference Learning" (BALD), arXiv:1112.5745, 2011.** https://arxiv.org/abs/1112.5745 (verified).
- Mechanism: choose the query x maximizing mutual information between the answer y and the model parameters θ: I[y;θ|x] = H[y|x] − E_{θ~posterior} H[y|x,θ]. For pairwise preference queries this is exactly the one-step expected information gain (EIG). Predictive entropy alone (H[y|x]) overcounts irreducible noise; the second term subtracts it, so BALD prefers questions where the *models disagree*, not where the person is simply inconsistent.
- IMPLEMENTATION HOOK: keep an ensemble/particle set of K parameter vectors (from 1.2 or A2). For each candidate question, compute p_k = P(answer|x,θ_k); score = H(mean_k p_k) − mean_k H(p_k) with `math.log2`. Ask only if score > τ_info AND expected cost (interruption) is acceptable. All in pure Python over a list of candidates.

**B2. Value-of-information framing of clarify-vs-commit.** "Value of Information: A Framework for Human–Agent Communication," arXiv:2601.06407 (2026) https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.06407v1 ; "Uncertainty-Aware Clarification in LLM Agents with Information Gain," arXiv:2606.03135 https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.03135 ; "Ask Early, Ask Late, Ask Right: When Does Clarification Timing Matter for Long-Horizon Agents?" arXiv:2605.07937 https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.07937 ; Adobe Research, "Structured Uncertainty-guided Clarification for LLM Agents" (uses Expected Value of Perfect Information over tool parameters) https://research.adobe.com/publication/structured-uncertainty-guided-clarification-for-llm-agents (all found via search; individual numbers UNVERIFIED).
- Mechanism: ask iff EVPI − cost_of_asking > 0, where EVPI = E_answer[ max_a U(a|answer) ] − max_a E[U(a)]. The VoI paper decomposes the decision into query ambiguity × task risk × cognitive load rather than a fixed confidence threshold; the timing paper shows *when* to ask matters as much as whether (ask before irreversible steps).
- **When to ask "why" (rationale) rather than "which":** ask "why" when (i) two rule-programs (1.2) explain the same choice with different *generalizations* (they disagree on unseen contexts even though they agree on observed ones — high BALD score on counterfactual contexts), or (ii) a drift detector (2D) fires and the model needs to know whether the change is a new stable policy or a one-off. A "why" answer is grounded (A4) as a constraint on θ or as a new predicate in the rule program; a "which" answer only adds one data point.
- IMPLEMENTATION HOOK: `questions.jsonl` with `{type: "which"|"why", trigger: "bald"|"drift"|"irreversible", eig, evpi, cost, asked, answer}`; enforce a budget (e.g. ≤3 questions/day) by sorting candidates by evpi−cost and taking the top ones. Log the pre-question predictive distribution so the question's actual information gain can be audited afterwards (realized KL).

### 2C. Calibration and prediction sets

**C1. Proper scoring rules.** Brier (1950); Murphy's decomposition: A.H. Murphy, "A New Vector Partition of the Probability Score," J. Appl. Meteorology 12:595–600, 1973 — Brier = Reliability − Resolution + Uncertainty. T. Gneiting & A.E. Raftery, "Strictly Proper Scoring Rules, Prediction, and Estimation," JASA 102(477):359–378, 2007 https://sites.stat.washington.edu/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf (verified via search): log score −log q_y and Brier Σ(q_k − 1[y=k])² are both strictly proper, i.e. minimized in expectation only by reporting true beliefs.
- IMPLEMENTATION HOOK: for every prediction row `{pred_dist, outcome}` compute log score and Brier in the benchmark script; report Murphy's three terms by binning predictions (10 bins): Reliability = Σ_b n_b (p̄_b − ō_b)², Resolution = Σ_b n_b (ō_b − ō)², Uncertainty = ō(1−ō). Log score should be the headline fidelity number because it punishes overconfident misses.

**C2. ECE and reliability diagrams.** C. Guo, G. Pleiss, Y. Sun, K.Q. Weinberger, "On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks," ICML 2017, PMLR 70:1321–1330. https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a.html , https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.04599 (verified).
- Mechanism: ECE = Σ_b (n_b/N) |acc(b) − conf(b)| over M confidence bins; reliability diagram plots acc(b) vs conf(b). Fix: **temperature scaling** — one scalar T fitted on a held-out set to minimize NLL of softmax(z/T); does not change argmax.
- IMPLEMENTATION HOOK: bins as a list of 10 `[sum_conf, sum_correct, n]` triples updated in a stream; fit T by 1-D golden-section search on held-out NLL (pure Python); store `T` in `calibration.json` and re-fit weekly. Publish ECE + the 10-bin table as the calibration line of the benchmark.

**C3. Conformal prediction sets.** A.N. Angelopoulos & S. Bates, "A Gentle Introduction to Conformal Prediction and Distribution-Free Uncertainty Quantification," arXiv:2107.07511 (2021). https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.07511 (verified).
- Mechanism (split conformal): on n calibration points compute nonconformity s_i = 1 − q̂(y_i | x_i); q̂_α = the ⌈(n+1)(1−α)⌉/n empirical quantile of {s_i}; prediction set C(x) = {y : 1 − q̂(y|x) ≤ q̂_α}. Guarantee: P(y ∈ C(x)) ≥ 1−α, distribution-free, assuming exchangeability. Under drift, exchangeability breaks — use a sliding calibration window or the adaptive-conformal update α_{t+1} = α_t + γ(α − err_t) (Gibbs & Candès 2021, cited in the same tutorial).
- IMPLEMENTATION HOOK: keep the last n=200 scored predictions in `conformal_window.jsonl`; sort scores with `sorted()`, index the quantile; emit a *set* of plausible actions per decision. **Act autonomously only if |C(x)| == 1**; otherwise defer or ask (ties this directly to 2B and 2I). Log coverage empirically and alarm when the rolling coverage falls below 1−α−0.05.

### 2D. Drift and change detection

**D1. ADWIN.** A. Bifet & R. Gavaldà, "Learning from Time-Changing Data with Adaptive Windowing," SIAM SDM 2007. https://www.cs.upc.edu/~gavalda/papers/adwin06.pdf (verified via search).
- Mechanism: keep a window W of recent values; for every split W = W0·W1 test |μ̂_W0 − μ̂_W1| ≥ ε_cut where ε_cut = sqrt( (1/(2m)) · ln(4/δ') ), m = 1/(1/|W0| + 1/|W1|) (harmonic mean of the sub-window sizes), δ' = δ/|W|; if it fires, drop W0 and repeat. Guarantees bounded false-positive rate (≤δ) and false-negative rate; ADWIN2 uses exponential-histogram buckets for O(log W) memory and amortized time. Only hyper-parameter is δ (0.002 typical).
- IMPLEMENTATION HOOK: apply to a stream of per-decision *fidelity scores* (1 if the clone's top prediction matched, else 0, or the log score) and separately to per-feature streams (reply latency, choice rates). Pure Python with `collections.deque` and the formula above; a fired detector writes `{ts, stream, old_mean, new_mean}` to `drift_events.jsonl` and triggers a 2B "why" question and a model re-fit on the retained window only.

**D2. CUSUM (Page 1954) and Page–Hinkley.** E.S. Page, "Continuous Inspection Schemes," Biometrika 41(1/2):100–115, 1954 (verified via search). Page–Hinkley as implemented in scikit-multiflow https://scikit-multiflow.readthedocs.io/en/stable/api/generated/skmultiflow.drift_detection.PageHinkley.html
- Mechanism: PH: m_T = Σ_{t≤T}(x_t − x̄_t − δ); M_T = min_{t≤T} m_t; alarm if m_T − M_T > λ. δ = minimum change of interest (default 0.005), λ = threshold (default 50 in skmultiflow; tune to your scale). Two-sided CUSUM: S⁺_t = max(0, S⁺_{t−1} + x_t − μ0 − k), S⁻_t = max(0, S⁻_{t−1} − x_t + μ0 − k); alarm if either > h; k ≈ half the shift you want to catch, h ≈ 4–5σ.
- IMPLEMENTATION HOOK: 6 lines of Python per detector; cheapest possible first line of defense. Run CUSUM on daily aggregates (style features, activity mix) with h=5σ and ADWIN on per-event streams.

**D3. Bayesian Online Changepoint Detection.** R.P. Adams & D.J.C. MacKay, "Bayesian Online Changepoint Detection," arXiv:0710.3742, 2007. https://arxiv.org/abs/0710.3742 (verified).
- Mechanism: maintain a distribution over run length r_t (time since last change). Recursion: P(r_t, x_{1:t}) = Σ_{r_{t−1}} P(r_t | r_{t−1}) · P(x_t | r_{t−1}, x^{(r)}) · P(r_{t−1}, x_{1:t−1}); the hazard H(r)=1/λ gives a constant change probability per step; the predictive P(x_t | run) comes from a conjugate model updated with sufficient statistics of that run (Beta–Bernoulli for match/mismatch streams; Normal–Gamma for latencies). Changepoint probability = P(r_t = 0). Gives a *posterior probability* of drift, not just an alarm — directly usable in calibrated uncertainty.
- IMPLEMENTATION HOOK: for Bernoulli fidelity streams the sufficient stats are (successes, failures) per run length — a list of tuples; prune run lengths with mass < 1e-4 to keep it O(few hundred). Store P(r_t=0) per day in `drift_posterior.jsonl`; the clone's confidence for a domain should be multiplied by (1 − P(recent change)) until enough post-change data has accumulated.

### 2E. Human self-consistency ceilings

**E1. Two-week survey test-retest (Park et al. 2024/2026, §1.1).** Participants re-answering the GSS two weeks later agreed with themselves ≈81% of the time (v1; recalled), Big Five self r≈0.95, economic-game self r near 0.99 on aggregated behavior but individual game choices noisier. **Twin-2K-500** (Toubia, Gui, Peng, Merlau, Li, Chen, arXiv:2505.17479; Marketing Science 2025 database report https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mksc.2025.0262 ) deliberately repeats wave-1 tasks in wave 4 "to establish a test-retest accuracy baseline" for 2,058 people × 500 questions (verified abstract; twin-accuracy numbers not on the abstract page — see the companion Mega-Study dataset https://huggingface.co/datasets/LLM-Digital-Twin/Twin-2K-500-Mega-Study , UNVERIFIED numbers).
**E2. Repeated incentivized choices are ~20–25% inconsistent.** J.D. Hey & C. Orme, "Investigating Generalizations of Expected Utility Theory Using Experimental Data," Econometrica 62(6):1291–1326, 1994 (verified via search): the same lottery pair presented twice yields a different choice a substantial fraction of the time; related literature reports mean error rates ≈22.7% (median 20%) across participants (secondary source via search; treat exact figure as UNVERIFIED). Also: "Does Repetition Improve Consistency?" Experimental Economics 4(1):5–54, 2001 (https://ideas.repec.org/a/kap/expeco/v4y2001i1p5-54.html).
- Decision relevance: **a clone cannot be expected to beat ~80% on attitudes or ~75–80% on repeated risky choices** because the person doesn't. Any fidelity metric must be normalized to the person's own retest, and the retest must be measured by the system itself.
- IMPLEMENTATION HOOK: schedule "retest probes": re-present ≈5% of past decisions (disguised/recontextualized) after ≥14 days; store `{orig_id, retest_answer}`; compute per-domain self-agreement; every fidelity report shows `model_agreement / self_agreement`. If self-agreement in a domain is < 0.6, mark the domain "inherently stochastic" and have the clone output distributions, never point predictions.

### 2F. Stylometry and blind identification

**F1. Burrows' Delta.** J.F. Burrows, "'Delta': a Measure of Stylistic Difference and a Guide to Likely Authorship," Literary and Linguistic Computing 17(3):267–287, 2002. https://academic.oup.com/dsh/article-abstract/17/3/267/929277 (verified). Reference reimplementation: https://github.com/pan-webis-de/burrows02
- Mechanism: take the N most frequent words in the corpus (N≈150–500, mostly function words); for each text compute relative frequency f_i, z-score it against corpus mean/sd: z_i = (f_i − μ_i)/σ_i; Delta(A,B) = (1/N) Σ_i |z_i(A) − z_i(B)|. Smallest Delta to a candidate's profile = attribution.

**F2. Which Delta works and why.** S. Evert, T. Proisl, F. Jannidis, I. Reger, S. Pielström, C. Schöch, T. Vitt, "Understanding and explaining Delta measures for authorship attribution," Digital Scholarship in the Humanities 32(suppl 2):ii4–ii16, 2017, doi:10.1093/llc/fqx023. https://academic.oup.com/dsh/article/32/suppl_2/ii4/3865676 (verified).
- Key finding: **vector-length normalization (i.e., Cosine Delta = angular distance between z-score vectors) is the decisive improvement**; Manhattan vs Euclidean matters less. Cosine Delta is robust to the number of features and to outlier words.
- IMPLEMENTATION HOOK: `style_profile.json` = {word: [mean, sd]} for the top-300 tokens of the person's own writing, plus per-text z-vectors; cosine distance via `sum(a*b)/(sqrt(sum a²)·sqrt(sum b²))`. A generated draft passes the style gate if its cosine Delta to the person's centroid is within the person's own intra-author distribution (e.g., ≤ 90th percentile of own-text distances).

**F3. Feature families and evaluation protocol.** E. Stamatatos, "A survey of modern authorship attribution methods," JASIST 60(3):538–556, 2009. https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/asi.21001 (verified). PAN shared tasks (Juola 2012 overview https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-1178/CLEF2012wn-PAN-Juola2012.pdf ; Stamatatos et al. 2014 https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-1180/CLEF2014wn-Pan-StamatosEt2014.pdf ) define the **authorship verification** framing (is this text by author X? yes/no) and the evaluation metrics (AUC, c@1 which rewards abstaining).
- Character n-grams (n=3–4) are the most robust single feature family across genres/languages because they capture morphology, punctuation and spacing habits; function-word frequencies are topic-independent.
- IMPLEMENTATION HOOK: verification, not attribution, is the right task for a clone: train a one-class profile (char-3/4-gram frequency dict + function-word z-vector) on the person's text; score a draft with cosine similarity; abstain (c@1) if within the ambiguous band. All dict/`collections.Counter` arithmetic.

**F4. Can LLMs imitate an individual's style? (blind tests).** (a) Z. Wang, N.I. Tripto, S. Park, Z. Li, J. Zhou, "Catch Me If You Can? Not Yet: LLMs Still Struggle to Imitate the Implicit Writing Styles of Everyday Authors," Findings of EMNLP 2025, arXiv:2509.14543 https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.14543 (verified): 400+ real authors, >40k generations/model, in-context few-shot from user samples; graded with an ensemble — authorship attribution, authorship verification, style matching, AI detection. **Result: adequate on structured genres (news, email), poor on informal blogs/forums** — implicit style not captured. (b) R. Jemama & R. Kumar, "How Well Do LLMs Imitate Human Writing Style?" arXiv:2509.24930 https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.24930 (verified): few-shot gives up to **23.5×** higher style-match accuracy than zero-shot; completion-style prompting reaches 99.9% style agreement on academic essays, **but** human perplexity ≈29.5 vs matched LLM output ≈15.2 — imitations are too predictable. Their detector: TF-IDF char n-grams + transformer embeddings, 97.5% accuracy.
- IMPLEMENTATION HOOK: the benchmark's "blind identification" line = a verification classifier (F3) + human 2-alternative forced-choice on 20 pairs; report both the classifier AUC (target: ≤0.6, i.e. near-indistinguishable) and the human hit rate (target: ≤60%). Also compute a simple unigram-perplexity proxy (type/token ratio, mean word-length variance, punctuation entropy) and reject drafts whose diversity is far below the person's own.

### 2G. Digital twins of individuals

**G1. Twin-2K-500.** O. Toubia, G.Z. Gui, T. Peng, D.J. Merlau, A. Li, H. Chen, "Twin-2K-500: A dataset for building digital twins of over 2,000 people based on their answers to over 500 questions," arXiv:2505.17479 (23 May 2025); Marketing Science 2025 (database report) https://pubsonline.informs.org/doi/10.1287/mksc.2025.0262 ; data https://huggingface.co/datasets/LLM-Digital-Twin/Twin-2K-500 (verified).
- Mechanism: 2,058 US respondents × 4 waves × ~500 items (2.42 h/person): demographics, psychology, personality, cognition, behavioral-economics replications, pricing; wave 4 repeats earlier tasks to give a test-retest ceiling. Twin-accuracy numbers are in the companion Mega-Study (UNVERIFIED here).
- IMPLEMENTATION HOOK: reuse its instrument categories as the schema of your "cold-start interview": 30–60 min of structured items across the same constructs, stored as JSONL `{construct, item, answer}`; it's the cheapest known way to bootstrap a twin before behavioral logs accumulate (compare Park's 2-hour interview).

**G2. Centaur.** M. Binz et al. (40 authors), "A foundation model to predict and capture human cognition," Nature 644:1002–1009, 28 Aug 2025; arXiv:2410.20268. https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-025-09215-4 , open access https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12390832/ (verified).
- Mechanism: Llama 3.1 70B + QLoRA (rank-8 adapters on all non-embedding layers, **0.15% of parameters**), one epoch on Psych-101 (160 experiments, 60,092 participants, 10,681,650 choices, 253.6M tokens). Prompt = the full trial-by-trial session of **a single participant**, so it conditions on the individual's history within a session.
- Numbers: held-out NLL 0.44 (Centaur) vs 0.58 (base Llama) vs 0.56 (best domain-specific cognitive models); beats cognitive models in all but one experiment; generalizes to modified cover stories (0.51 vs 0.63), altered task structure (0.42 vs 0.62) and a new domain, logical reasoning (1.65 vs 1.92).
- Decision relevance: a *tiny* adapter conditioned on within-session history beats hand-built cognitive models — evidence that "history in context + light adaptation" is enough for behavioral prediction. But Centaur is a population model that conditions on a session; it does not persist an individual across sessions — that is exactly the gap a personal clone fills.
- IMPLEMENTATION HOOK: the stdlib analogue is "condition the LLM prompt on the person's recent decision trajectory in the same domain" (retrieval of the last k decisions from JSONL) rather than a global persona paragraph.

**G3. Persona / user simulators.** S. Moon, M. Abdulhai, M. Kang, J. Suh, W. Soedarmadji, E. Kohen Behar, D.M. Chan, "Virtual Personas for Language Models via an Anthology of Backstories," EMNLP 2024 main, arXiv:2407.06576 https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.1110/ (verified): conditioning on open-ended life narratives ("backstories") yields up to **18%** better match to human response distributions and **27%** better consistency on Pew ATP surveys vs demographic personas. Critique: Q. Wang et al., "LLM-based Human Simulations Have Not Yet Been Reliable," arXiv:2501.08579 (v3 Jul 2026) https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.08579 — attributes unreliability to LLM limits + simulation-design flaws; proposes data-enrichment / capability / design framework. Survey: "User Simulation in the Era of Generative AI," arXiv:2501.04410 https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.04410 .
- IMPLEMENTATION HOOK: store the person's own narrative answers as the backstory block (Park's interview and Anthology's backstories agree that *first-person narrative beats demographic tags*); regenerate the block from `reflections.jsonl` weekly so it tracks drift.

### 2H. Cognitive architectures for agent memory

**H1. CoALA.** T.R. Sumers, S. Yao, K. Narasimhan, T.L. Griffiths, "Cognitive Architectures for Language Agents," TMLR 2024; arXiv:2309.02427 https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.02427 (verified).
- Mechanism: memory split into working memory + long-term (episodic, semantic, procedural); action space split into internal (reasoning, retrieval, learning = writes to memory) and external; decision cycle = plan (propose, evaluate, select) → execute. Procedural memory is where a policy lives.
- IMPLEMENTATION HOOK: map directly: `episodic/*.jsonl` (raw events), `semantic/profile.json` (facts/preferences), `procedural/rules.json` (1.2 programs); the decision loop is a Python function whose "learning" actions are appends to those files, making every memory write auditable.

**H2. Generative Agents (memory stream + reflection).** J.S. Park, J. O'Brien, C.J. Cai, M.R. Morris, P. Liang, M.S. Bernstein, "Generative Agents: Interactive Simulacra of Human Behavior," UIST 2023, arXiv:2304.03442 https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3586183.3606763 (verified).
- Mechanism: retrieval score = α_rec·recency + α_imp·importance + α_rel·relevance (all α=1); recency = exponential decay (0.995/hour in the reference implementation), importance = LLM-rated 1–10, relevance = embedding cosine. **Reflection** triggers when the sum of importance of recent events exceeds a threshold (150) — about 2–3 times/day — producing higher-level memories that are themselves retrievable.
- IMPLEMENTATION HOOK: recency and importance are stdlib; replace embedding cosine with BM25/Jaccard over tokens (or a cached LLM-embedding call stored in JSON). Reflection threshold on cumulative importance is a good budget rule for when to spend an LLM call on summarization.

**H3. A-MEM.** W. Xu, Z. Liang, K. Mei, H. Gao, J. Tan, Y. Zhang, "A-MEM: Agentic Memory for LLM Agents," arXiv:2502.12110 (2025) https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.12110 , code https://github.com/agiresearch/a-mem (verified).
- Mechanism: Zettelkasten-style notes — each memory gets `{content, context description, keywords, tags, links}`; new notes trigger link creation to similar old notes and **memory evolution** (old notes' descriptions/tags updated in light of the new one). Reported up to 6× on multi-hop reasoning and 85–93% fewer memory-op tokens vs baselines.
- IMPLEMENTATION HOOK: `notes.jsonl` with `links:[ids]`; linking by keyword/tag overlap (Jaccard ≥ θ); evolution = re-summarize affected notes lazily with a version counter so old versions stay on disk for audit.

**H4. MemGPT.** C. Packer, S. Wooders, K. Lin, V. Fang, S.G. Patil, I. Stoica, J.E. Gonzalez, "MemGPT: Towards LLMs as Operating Systems," arXiv:2310.08560 (2023) https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.08560 (verified).
- Mechanism: main context (system + working + FIFO message queue) vs external context (recall storage, archival storage); the LLM itself issues function calls to page data in/out; interrupts/yields manage control flow; evaluated on document QA and multi-session chat consistency.
- IMPLEMENTATION HOOK: treat the prompt as a fixed-size budget; expose two tools `recall_search(query)` and `archive_append(note)` backed by JSONL + a tiny inverted index; keep a `core_memory.json` (≤2 KB persona + human blocks) that the model may edit only through an explicit, logged tool call.

### 2I. Shadow mode, champion–challenger, off-policy evaluation

**I1. Shadow (dark-launch) deployment.** Industry pattern rather than a paper: C. Samiullah, "Deploying Machine Learning Models in Shadow Mode," 2019 https://christophergs.com/machine%20learning/2019/03/30/deploying-machine-learning-applications-in-shadow-mode/ ; Atlan, "Shadow Deployment for ML Models" https://atlan.com/know/shadow-deployment-for-ml-models/ (verified via search).
- Mechanism: mirror every live request to the challenger; log its output; the champion's output is what acts; compare offline on agreement, latency, drift. Promote only after a pre-declared window and criteria.
- IMPLEMENTATION HOOK: `shadow.jsonl` rows `{ctx_id, champion_action, challenger_action, human_action, ts}`; the human's actual decision is the ground truth; a challenger is promoted when its rolling log-score beats the champion's over ≥N decisions **and** ADWIN has not fired on the human stream. Every clone version starts in shadow.

**I2. Doubly-robust off-policy evaluation.** M. Dudík, J. Langford, L. Li, "Doubly Robust Policy Evaluation and Learning," ICML 2011 https://icml.cc/2011/papers/554_icmlpaper.pdf ; journal version Statistical Science 29(4), 2014 (verified). Sequential extension: N. Jiang & L. Li, "Doubly Robust Off-policy Value Evaluation for Reinforcement Learning," ICML 2016, PMLR 48 https://proceedings.mlr.press/v48/jiang16.html (verified). Foundation: D. Precup, R.S. Sutton, S. Singh, "Eligibility Traces for Off-Policy Policy Evaluation," ICML 2000 https://web.eecs.umich.edu/~baveja/Papers/OffPolicy.pdf (verified).
- Mechanism (bandit form): V̂_DR = (1/n) Σ_i [ r̂(x_i, π(x_i)) + (1[a_i = π(x_i)] / p_i) · (r_i − r̂(x_i, a_i)) ], where p_i is the logging propensity of the taken action and r̂ a reward model. Unbiased if *either* p or r̂ is correct; variance far below plain IPS. Per-decision importance sampling (Precup 2000) extends to trajectories: weight = Π_t π(a_t|s_t)/μ(a_t|s_t).
- Decision relevance: lets you estimate "how well would the clone's policy have done" from logs of what the human did, **before** the clone ever acts — but only if the logging policy (the human) is stochastic enough that the clone's actions have support; where p_i=0 you cannot evaluate, which is another reason to keep the conformal-set (2C) abstain rule.
- IMPLEMENTATION HOOK: the human's action distribution p_i is estimated from the fitted Boltzmann model (A3); reward = outcome label or user-satisfaction proxy; compute V̂_DR over `shadow.jsonl` in pure Python; report with a bootstrap CI (resample rows 200×).

### 2J. Authorization primitives for an agent acting as a person

**J1. UCAN v1.0.0.** UCAN Working Group, "User Controlled Authorization Network Specification," https://github.com/ucan-wg/spec , https://ucan.xyz/specification/ (verified).
- Mechanism: signed tokens with `iss`, `aud`, `sub`, `cmd` (e.g. `/crud/read`), `args`, policy, `exp`, `nbf`, nonce, and a proof chain; **attenuation rule**: each delegation must restate or narrow the capability; **invocation** is a separate signed object with a CID that exercises the chain; **revocation**: any issuer in the chain may revoke, breaking all downstream delegations; recommended short expirations.
- IMPLEMENTATION HOOK: the person is the root issuer; each clone version gets a UCAN-shaped JSON `{iss, aud, sub, cmd, args-policy, exp, nonce, proof}` signed with `hmac`/`hashlib` (stdlib) as a local stand-in for DID keys; a `revocations.jsonl` checked before every invocation.

**J2. Macaroons.** A. Birgisson, J.G. Politz, Ú. Erlingsson, A. Taly, M. Vrable, M. Lentczner, "Macaroons: Cookies with Contextual Caveats for Decentralized Authorization in the Cloud," NDSS 2014, doi:10.14722/ndss.2014.23212 https://research.google/pubs/pub41892/ (verified).
- Mechanism: bearer token = HMAC chain; sig_0 = HMAC(root_key, id); adding caveat c_i → sig_i = HMAC(sig_{i−1}, c_i). Anyone holding a macaroon can **only attenuate** (add caveats: time < T, action ∈ {…}, amount ≤ X, third-party discharge required) and never widen; verifier recomputes the chain with the root key. Third-party caveats give "requires fresh human approval" as a discharge macaroon.
- IMPLEMENTATION HOOK: 30 lines with `hmac`+`hashlib.sha256`; caveats as JSON strings; per-task macaroons minted by the owner with `expires`, `scope`, `max_amount` caveats; every invocation logs the caveat list — this is the audit trail.

**J3. OAuth delegation and revocation.** RFC 8693 "OAuth 2.0 Token Exchange" (delegation semantics via the `act` claim and `may_act`; nested `act` claims record a delegation chain) https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc8693.html ; RFC 9396 "Rich Authorization Requests" (`authorization_details` JSON array for fine-grained, typed scopes) https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9396.html ; RFC 7009 "Token Revocation" https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7009.html (all verified).
- Mechanism: an agent acting *on behalf of* a user should hold a token whose subject is the user and whose `act` claim is the agent — this is the standards-based way to make "the clone did this, for this person" visible to every relying party. RAR lets you express "send email to ≤3 recipients in domain X, no attachments" rather than a flat `mail.send` scope.
- IMPLEMENTATION HOOK: even for local tools, emit an `act`-style JSON envelope `{sub: person, act: {sub: "clone-v7"}, authorization_details:[...]}` in every action log line; refuse to execute when the envelope is missing or expired.

**J4. EU AI Act Article 50 (applies from 2 Aug 2026).** Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, Art. 50 https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/ ; Commission FAQ https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/faqs/transparency-obligations-under-article-50-ai-act ; draft Code of Practice on Transparency of AI-Generated Content (first draft 17 Dec 2025) https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/code-practice-ai-generated-content (verified).
- Obligations: (1) people interacting with an AI system must be told so, unless obvious; (2) providers must mark synthetic audio/image/video/**text** outputs in a machine-readable, detectable way; (3) notice for emotion-recognition/biometric categorization; (4) deployers must disclose deepfakes (Art. 3(60): content resembling real persons that would falsely appear authentic) and AI-generated text published on matters of public interest without human review; (5) disclosure must be clear at the latest at first interaction.
- Decision relevance: **a clone that writes or speaks as a real person is squarely the "resembles existing persons" case.** Messages the clone sends must carry a machine-readable AI-origin mark and, for external recipients, a human-readable disclosure at first interaction unless the person reviewed/edited the text (the human-review exception in 50(4)).
- IMPLEMENTATION HOOK: every outbound artifact gets a header/footer field `{ai_generated: true, model: "...", reviewed_by_human: bool, ts}` stored alongside the message in `outbox.jsonl`; a policy check refuses to send external messages without either `reviewed_by_human=true` or a visible disclosure line.

## 15-line summary of decision-relevant findings
1. The fidelity ceiling is the person, not 100%: two-week self-agreement on attitudes ≈80%; repeated risky choices ≈20–25% inconsistent. Always report `model_agreement / self_agreement`, and measure the retest yourself (Park 2411.10109; Twin-2K-500; Hey–Orme 1994).
2. Interview-grounded agents reach 83–86% of that ceiling on GSS attitudes but only ≈0.66 normalized on incentivized behavior; expect a large attitude→behavior gap and benchmark the two separately.
3. First-person narrative (interview/backstory) beats demographic/persona tags every time it has been tested (Park; Anthology +18%/+27%); a 30–60-minute structured cold-start interview is the cheapest bootstrap.
4. Behavior-as-code (ROTE) gives ~50% over behavior cloning with sparse data and transfers to new contexts: represent habits as small rule programs with a particle posterior (ε-likelihood, SMC rejuvenation). Stdlib-implementable.
5. Rewards are not identifiable from behavior (Lazzati–Metelli): store the feasible set / centroid and flag decisions where the set is wide instead of pretending to a point utility.
6. One likelihood for all feedback channels: reward-rational implicit choice (Jeon 2020) + Boltzmann β per person (Baker 2009); fitted β is both an interpretable "consistency" knob and a drift signal.
7. Ask only when models disagree, not when the person is noisy: BALD/EIG over a parameter ensemble; ask "why" (not "which") when two programs agree on observed data but diverge on counterfactuals, or right after a drift alarm; gate all questions by EVPI − cost and a daily budget.
8. Headline uncertainty metric = log score (strictly proper); publish ECE + reliability bins; fit one temperature scalar weekly.
9. Act autonomously only when the split-conformal prediction set at 1−α is a singleton; otherwise defer/ask. Under drift use a sliding calibration window or adaptive-α.
10. Drift stack: CUSUM on daily aggregates (cheap), ADWIN on per-event fidelity streams (bounded false alarms, one δ), BOCPD for a *posterior* change probability that discounts confidence after a change.
11. Long-context prompting degrades as preferences become implicit (RealPref); extract preferences to a structured store at ingest and tag by expression type.
12. Memory: three tiers (episodes → reflections → rules) per the ACL 2026 survey and CoALA; reflection triggered by cumulative importance (Generative Agents); A-MEM-style links/evolution; MemGPT-style explicit, logged core-memory edits.
13. Style: verification not attribution; Cosine Delta on top-300 words + char 3/4-grams; LLM imitation is adequate for email/news but fails on informal text and is too low-perplexity — add a diversity check and a blind 2AFC human test to the benchmark.
14. Never let a new clone version act first: shadow-log champion vs challenger vs the human's real action, evaluate with doubly-robust OPE from the fitted Boltzmann propensities, promote only when log score wins and no drift alarm is active.
15. Authority must be attenuable, expiring, revocable and auditable (macaroons/UCAN semantics, OAuth `act` + RAR), and from 2 Aug 2026 EU AI Act Art. 50 requires machine-readable AI-origin marking of clone-generated text and disclosure to counterparts unless a human reviewed it.
